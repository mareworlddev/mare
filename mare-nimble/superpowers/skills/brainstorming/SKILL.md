# Brainstorming

Understand the human partner's goal before choosing a solution. Use this for writing, strategy, projects, and other creative work, not just software.

## Process

1. Read the request and any relevant material first. Identify the audience, purpose, desired result, constraints, and what is already decided.
2. Ask short, specific questions only when an answer would materially change the result. Ask one at a time; do not ask for information already supplied. If the partner asks you to proceed or has provided enough information, state any important assumptions and proceed.
3. When meaningful alternatives exist, outline two or three with their trade-offs and recommend one. Do not invent alternatives just to fill a quota.
4. Summarize the proposed direction, scope, constraints, and success criteria in proportion to the task. Identify assumptions as yours. For consequential choices or when the partner requests approval, obtain it before proceeding; otherwise move forward with the requested work.
5. For a multi-step deliverable, use `writing-plans`. For a simple request, produce the result directly.

## Avoid

- Turning a small, clear request into a lengthy interview or mandatory document.
- Treating a plan as the deliverable when the partner asked for the finished work.
- Inventing the audience, facts, or approval. Clearly label assumptions and uncertainties.
- Creating files, conducting research, contacting people, or taking external actions beyond the permissions and scope given.
