# Verification Before Completion

Evidence before claims. Check the actual result against the partner's request and available source material before saying it is complete.

## Verification gate

1. Identify each requirement and constraint: purpose, audience, format, tone, length, required sections, sources, calculations, and other explicit rules.
2. Inspect the finished deliverable itself against each applicable requirement. For data or research, check calculations, dates, citations, source support, and consistency with provided documents. For prose, check structure, tone, contradictions, and word or character limits when specified.
3. Perform the strongest feasible check for each claim: reread the source, recalculate, inspect the output, or run a relevant test. Do not treat an agent's report, a previous result, or a plausible answer as proof.
4. Correct problems within scope, then recheck affected requirements. If information, permissions, tools, or time prevent a check, state what remains unverified and its impact.
5. Report the actual result and evidence proportionately. Never say a requirement passed when it was not checked, or that all work is complete if a required part is missing.

## Examples

- An article with a 500-word limit: count the final text and compare its claims to the supplied sources.
- A market analysis: check the arithmetic and whether each conclusion is supported by the cited evidence; label estimates and gaps.
- A delegated research task: inspect the returned sources and synthesis rather than trusting the reported completion.
