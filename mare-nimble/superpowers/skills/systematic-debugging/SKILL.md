# Systematic Debugging

Investigate before recommending a remedy. Distinguish observations from assumptions, correlation from causation, and a supported cause from an untested hypothesis. Scale the investigation to the risk and the available evidence.

## Four phases

### 1. Investigate the problem

Define the expected and actual outcomes, when and where the difference occurs, its scale, and who is affected. Inspect available evidence, data quality, and relevant changes. Trace inputs and handoffs through the process to locate where the result changes. Record missing evidence; do not claim a root cause without support.

### 2. Analyze patterns

Compare affected and unaffected periods, segments, teams, or cases using comparable measures. Look for differences, dependencies, and alternative explanations, including seasonal effects or changes in measurement. A correlation is a lead, not proof.

### 3. Form and test hypotheses

State a specific hypothesis, its supporting evidence, and what observation would challenge it. Choose the smallest safe and authorized check that can distinguish it from plausible alternatives. Review the result; if it fails, revise the hypothesis or gather more evidence. Do not fabricate measurements or imply an observational comparison proves causality.

### 4. Act and verify

Once evidence supports a cause, recommend or implement a proportionate action within the partner's authorization. Define how and when to check whether the original outcome improved, and watch for unintended effects. If the action cannot be tested yet, provide the verification plan and say so. Escalate high-impact or irreversible actions before taking them when approval is required.

## When evidence is insufficient

State what is known, the leading hypotheses and their uncertainty, and the next evidence or safe check needed. Offer provisional mitigations when appropriate, explicitly labeled as such. If immediate action is necessary to limit harm, separate a reversible mitigation from the continuing investigation; do not present it as a confirmed fix.

**Example:** A sales decline may reflect fewer leads, lower conversion, or reporting changes. Compare the funnel by period and segment, validate tracking, and test the leading explanation before recommending a budget shift.
