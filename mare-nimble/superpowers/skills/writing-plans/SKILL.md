# Writing Plans

Turn the request and available context into a practical route to the requested result. Match the plan's detail to the task; a short outline may be enough.

## Build the plan

1. Establish the objective, audience, requested output, constraints, source material, and definition of success. State unresolved assumptions; ask only when a missing decision would change the result materially.
2. Break the work into ordered, meaningful stages. For each, specify the output, necessary inputs or dependencies, and a concrete way to check it. Include a responsible person or deadline only when known or useful.
3. For writing, outline the argument and sections before drafting each part. For research or strategy, describe the questions, evidence needed, and how findings will be synthesized. For projects, include milestones and dependencies when relevant.
4. Review the plan against the request: every requirement must have an owner or stage, and no step should be unnecessary. Note risks and information gaps without presenting guesses as facts.
5. Present the plan and proceed to the requested deliverable unless the partner explicitly asks to review first, an essential decision is missing, or an action needs authorization. Adapt the plan when evidence changes; explain consequential deviations.

A plan is not completion when the partner asked for the final article, analysis, or project outcome. Do not create a separate plan file unless useful or requested.
